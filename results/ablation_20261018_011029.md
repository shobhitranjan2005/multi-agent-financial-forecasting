## 1. Descriptive Findings: Market Base Rates, Model Bias, and Reference Floors

- **Test Set Market Base Rate**: Over the 68 frozen post-cutoff evaluation cases, **40 of 68 outcomes (58.82%) were 'down'** and 28 of 68 (41.18%) were 'up'. Consequently, an uninformative **always-down rule would score 58.82% directional accuracy**, while the buy-and-hold proxy (`naive-alwaysup`) scored only 41.18%.
- **Baseline Bullish Bias**: The single-LLM baseline (`baseline`) predicted **'up' on 66 of 68 cases (97.06%)**, predicting 'down' only twice. Its directional accuracy of 41.18% is entirely an artifact of matching the 41.18% 'up' base rate of the market period.
- **Multi-Agent Moderation**: In contrast, the multi-agent system (`multiagent`) moderated this extreme bullish bias to **44 'up' calls and 24 'down' calls (64.71% up)**, while `multiagent-nodebate` produced 55 'up' calls and 13 'down' calls (80.88% up).
- **Brier No-Skill Reference Floor**: An uncalibrated 50/50 probability forecast yields an expected **Brier score of exactly 0.2500** ($0.5^2$). The single-LLM baseline achieved a Brier score of **0.2787** (worse than the 0.25 reference floor; overconfidence gap 18.23 pts). Multi-agent without debate achieved **0.2880** (overconfidence gap 19.48 pts). Full multi-agent with debate achieved **0.2593** (overconfidence gap 10.69 pts), bringing probability estimates closest to the reference baseline.
